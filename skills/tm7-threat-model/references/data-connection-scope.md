## 1. Data Connection

Article 2(1) states that the CRA `applies to products with digital elements made available on the market, the intended purpose or reasonably foreseeable use of  which includes a direct or indirect logical or physical data connection to a device or network`. The scope of the CRA is anchored not in the mere presence of electronics, but in the capacity of a product with digital elements to exchange digital information.

Legal basis [CRA Regulation (EU) 2024/2847](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=OJ:L_202402847), Article 2(1) (Scope), Article 3 (Definitions), and [Commission guidance C(2026) 5252, Annex](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/commission-publishes-new-guidance-support-timely-cyber-resilience-act-implementation), published 27 July 2026, Section 2.5, paragraphs 26–28 (Data connection). Treat the guidance as non-binding interpretation, distinct from the Regulation.

| Physical connection            | Article 3(9)                                                                 | A connection between electronic information systems or components implemented by physical means, including electrical, optical or mechanical interfaces, wires or radio waves.                             | Ethernet, USB, serial interfaces (UART, SPI, I²C), Bluetooth, Wi-Fi, optical communication.                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| Indirect connection            | Article 3(10)                                                                | A connection to a device or network that occurs as part of a larger system directly connectable to that device or network.                                                                                 | An embedded controller exchanging digital data with a connected host system; software integrated into a network-connected appliance.                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| Direct connection              | Article 2(1); analytical interpretation, not separately defined in Article 3 | A connection of the assessed product to the specified device or network without relying on the product's participation in a larger system in the sense described above.                                    | Direct USB communication with a computer; an Ethernet-enabled device connecting to a network; a Bluetooth device communicating with another device.                                                                                                                                                                    |
| Data connection / digital data | Article 2(1); Commission Guidance §2.5, paragraphs 26–28                     | Digital information is deliberately encoded by a sender using an interpretable representation (e.g. binary symbols), transmitted through a connection, and capable of being decoded as data by a receiver. | UART messages, CAN frames and encoded sensor readings illustrate digital communication. For discrete I/O, document what the states represent and how the receiving input interprets them. Signals used solely to trigger or power a function without conveying encoded digital information do not meet this criterion. |

## 2. Scope Decision Procedure

1. Record the assessment objective, product boundary, intended purpose, reasonably foreseeable use, and available architecture evidence. Apply the CRA connection criterion only for CRA-aligned work; a general threat model still needs explicit boundaries and exclusions.
2. Establish whether the interface conveys digital information using section 1.1. Record the sender, receiver, representation, and interpretation evidence. Do not infer digital communication solely from a connector name or a high/low electrical state. Treat missing evidence as unresolved, not as evidence of absence.
3. For an evidenced data connection, classify path, type, and target using C1–C8, relative to the stated assessed boundary and endpoints. State the viewpoint: a logical interaction and its physical bearer can have separate classifications. Document intermediaries and map both directions where applicable. No C1–C8 case is an exclusion category.
4. Decide model inclusion separately. Keep external interactions and dependencies visible when they can affect the assessed product, even when the external component's internals are excluded. An absent digital-data criterion for one interface does not establish that the whole product is outside CRA scope, or that a physical threat is irrelevant. A complete regulatory applicability decision requires the other applicable scope conditions and exclusions beyond this connection taxonomy.
5. For each proposed exclusion, identify its basis: evidenced absence of a data connection, assessment-boundary limitation, or evidenced absent/eliminated attack-path precondition. State exactly what is excluded and what remains covered. Do not use physical access requirements, indirect connectivity, a trusted environment, or compensating controls alone as an exclusion reason.
6. Keep unresolved exclusions pending. In TM7, do not set `Out Of Scope` to true merely because evidence is missing; preserve an existing value during editing but flag an unsupported inherited exclusion for reconciliation before relying on it. In analysis, use the mode-aware gates and `Needs Investigation` where the gap affects a row. Do not infer `Not Applicable`, `Avoidance`, zero impact, or residual risk `None` from an assessment limitation alone.

## 3. Decision Record and Handoff

Record a stable decision ID, rule revision and repository commit, assessment boundary, element/flow UUIDs and direction, connection classification (or the evidence that it does not apply), inclusion/exclusion decision, reason category, supporting evidence references, assumptions, and unresolved questions. For an intentionally unmodeled interface, record its name and endpoints without inventing a native TMT UUID. Link any subsequent native threat row IDs to the decision during analysis.

In TM7, place the concise decision ID, exclusion rationale, and evidence reference in the existing `Reason For Out Of Scope` property when `Out Of Scope` is true. Keep the complete record in supported model assumptions/notes or accompanying scope documentation. When only an external component's internal decomposition is excluded, record that limitation in assumptions/notes; do not mark the external-interactor stencil or its retained product-facing flows out of scope. Do not invent XML members or change the Core catalog. Include intentionally omitted return paths and excluded items even if they produce no exported threat rows.

In analysis, read the TM7 scope properties and accompanying records in addition to the CSV; do not assume the CSV carries element-level exclusions. Validate the inherited decision against current evidence and the recorded rule revision. Reference the decision in the narrative `Justification` and summary without changing the fixed CSV schema. Analysis owns threat applicability, scoring, treatment, and approval; a model exclusion is not an automatic threat disposition.

When new evidence changes scope, record the old decision, new evidence, revised conclusion, and affected model elements/rows. Preserve existing source rows. If relevant threats were omitted, return to model revision and obtain a new TMT export before including them in the analysis CSV; track the gap in the summary meanwhile. Do not invent rows to compensate for an incomplete export.

## 4. Consistency Examples

| Evidence and viewpoint | Model decision | Analysis consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A workstation sends device-specific commands through a gateway. | Classify the mediated logical device interaction as C5; retain the path. | Evaluate applicable threats; indirect connectivity is not an exclusion. |
| A discrete input's encoding and receiving behavior are undocumented. | Keep classification unresolved; request interface evidence. | Use `Needs Investigation` for affected rows, not `Not Applicable`. |
| Evidence shows one connection only supplies power; the product also has a digital service port. | Record the non-data finding for the power interface only; retain the service path and relevant physical threats. | Do not conclude that the product or all threats are outside scope. |
| A supplier workstation's internals are outside the product boundary, but it sends firmware updates. | Exclude only its internal decomposition; retain the update interaction. | Assess the product-facing update threat and document the boundary limitation. |
| A response direction is intentionally omitted from a limited assessment. | Record the omitted direction and limitation explicitly. | Do not infer that response threats are impossible; request expanded modeling when relevant. |
| Design evidence confirms the candidate debug interface is absent and that interface is a necessary precondition of the specific candidate path. | Record the architectural evidence and exact rejected path. | `Not Applicable`/`Avoidance` may be supported for that scenario; do not generalize to other interfaces. |
